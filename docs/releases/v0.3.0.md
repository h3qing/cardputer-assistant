## v0.3.0 · A pocket Granola: voice memos and meeting recording

From this release the Cardputer can **record offline**: hold G0 to record a voice memo, or press `Opt+R` to record a whole meeting. When plugged into the Mac, recordings are sent over automatically, stored in `~/CardputerNotes/recordings/`, and handed to WhisperWoof for transcription, a summary and to-dos.

### New features
- **Voice memo**: hold G0, start talking after the beep, release to stop. Measured from press to recording start: about 0.59 s.
- **Meeting recording**: press `Opt+R`; the screen says "Tell the people present first", and recording starts only after Enter. The start and stop tones cannot be turned off; the screen shows ● REC while recording; press G0 during a meeting to add a ★ mark.
- **Recorded on the SD card**: 16 kHz mono WAV, split seamlessly into segments. Each segment has a `.M` commit marker, so it recovers after power loss. Recording and transfer never run at the same time.
- **Automatic transfer to the Mac**: a new `file.*` protocol, with a CRC on every chunk and resumable transfers; the device deletes a file only after the Mac confirms it. Measured on the real device at about 330 KB/s, so a 1-hour recording transfers in about 5.6 minutes.
- **Mac side (cpad)**: pulls recordings and checks them with SHA-256, writes them as `~/CardputerNotes/recordings/<date>/<time>-<session>/` folders with a manifest per recording contract v1; waits for WhisperWoof's output; adds a line to that day's note; **audio is kept for 30 days, text forever**; new `cpa rec ls` and `cpa rec show`.
- **Launcher installer** (`tools/launcher`): one command downloads, verifies and flashes Launcher, then installs each app by name.

### Fixes
- **SD driver**: at the end of a multi-block write, chip select was released before the card finished being busy, so a single file failed with EIO at about 8 MB. After the fix, writing 9.6 MB and reading it back passed 3/3, and 16 MB passed 5/5.
- **SD card fails to mount after a reset interrupts a write**: new `sd_wake` first finishes the interrupted write at boot, then initialises. We reproduced the fault with a control firmware.
- A stack overflow in the device tests; the number of simultaneously open files is back to 2 (heap used by the mount back to 15 KB); the version number of the v0.3 firmware.
- A recording session cut short by power loss is now archived within the same connection, without waiting for the next one.

### Verification
- 356 protocol tests (31 vectors for the recording contract); 408 firmware native tests, 97% coverage; 836 daemon tests, 96.8% coverage; 229 HIL tool tests; 227 Launcher tool tests.
- Real device:
  - All 20 device test cases pass. Memory: the full app has 190 KB heap free when idle, 271 KB while recording.
  - Recording HIL 6/6 passed, covering voice memos, meetings, resume after disconnect, refusing transfer while recording, recovery after reboot, segmenting, and a 5-minute long run.
  - Before the fix, a 10-minute meeting long run was also done: reply latency p95 was 22.5 ms, and every segment's sample count matched exactly.
- v0.2 regression: with card 14/14, without card 11/11, migration passed.

### Dependencies and known issues
- **Transcription and summaries wait for WhisperWoof PRs**: h3qing/WhisperWoof #149–#152 need the user to review and merge them, and then turn on the Cardputer inbox in WhisperWoof's settings. Until they are merged, recordings still reach `~/CardputerNotes/recordings/` but are not transcribed.
- **Checks still to be done by the user** (#23): microphone level and recognition accuracy for mixed Chinese and English speech, tone volume, battery life while recording, power loss during recording, card removal during recording, and an end-to-end test with the real WhisperWoof. The microphone gain and silence threshold are still provisional.
- Not rerun since the fix: the segmenting test, the 10-minute recording long run, migration (#22); debug counters not yet filled in in the firmware (#21).
- **#14**: plugging in with the switch already ON, the Mac does not detect the device.
